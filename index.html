ACTIVE ARCHIVE: THE MOON
Mass & Fuel Trade-Off

Why Did Equipment Have to Be Left Behind?

The reason lies in mass. Every extra kilogram on the Lunar Module meant additional fuel consumption during liftoff from the lunar surface.

Before launch, astronauts unloaded everything they could do without on the return trip: cameras, sleeping hammocks, geological tools, life-support backpacks, lunar overshoes, and waste containers. The more equipment they left behind, the more lunar rock and soil samples they could bring back to Earth. Six descent stages and five Saturn V third stages remain on the Moon today.

03 / A SCIENCE LEGACY, STILL REACHABLE

A signal sent
across time.

Some equipment left behind still supports science. Lunar laser retroreflectors deployed by Apollo 11, Apollo 14, and Apollo 15 allow researchers on Earth to measure the distance to the Moon within a few centimeters by timing reflected laser pulses — proving that the Moon recedes from Earth at 3.8 cm per year.
